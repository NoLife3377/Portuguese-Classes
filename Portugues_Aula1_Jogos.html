🗺️ Where is Portugal?

Portugal is in the far southwest of Europe, on the Iberian Peninsula. Its only neighbour by land is Spain (a Espanha), to the north and east. To the west and south is the Atlantic Ocean. The capital is Lisboa (Lisbon). Other important cities: Porto (north), Coimbra (centre) and Faro (south, the Algarve). About 10 million people live there, and the currency is the euro (€).

Mainland Portugal has regions such as the North (Porto), the Centre (Coimbra), Lisbon, the Alentejo and the Algarve in the south. There are also two island regions in the Atlantic — Madeira 🌺 and the Azores 🌋 (os Açores). The longest river is the Tejo (Tagus), which reaches the sea at Lisbon. The climate is mild, with warm, sunny summers. 🌞

📜 A short history

  • Before 1143The Romans called this land Lusitânia. Later came the Visigoths and, from 711, the Moors (Muslims from North Africa).
  • 1143Portugal becomes an independent kingdom — the first king is Dom Afonso Henriques.
  • 1249The borders are fixed — almost the same as today. Portugal is one of Europe’s oldest countries.
  • 1400s–1500sThe Age of Discoveries — Portuguese ships explore the oceans.
  • 1580–1640Portugal and Spain share the same king; in 1640 Portugal restores its independence.
  • 1755A huge earthquake and tsunami destroy much of Lisbon.
  • 1910Portugal becomes a republic.
  • 1974The Carnation Revolution (25 de Abril) brings democracy.
  • 1986Portugal joins the European Union; it has used the euro since 2002.

⛵ The Discoveries

to India — around Africa (1498)   to Brazil (1500)

It began with Infante Dom Henrique (“Henry the Navigator”), who sent ships south along the coast of Africa. Sailors (os navegadores) crossed the oceans in small ships called caravelas, guided by the stars and the compass.

Key voyages: Bartolomeu Dias rounded the south of Africa (the Cape of Good Hope) in 1488; Vasco da Gama reached India by sea in 1498; Pedro Álvares Cabral arrived in Brazil in 1500; and Fernão de Magalhães (Magellan) began the first voyage around the whole world (1519–1522). This is why Portuguese is spoken on several continents today.

🇵🇹 The flag

🟩 verde (green) · 🟥 vermelho (red) · esfera armilar (armillary sphere) · escudo (shield)

The flag (a bandeira) was adopted in 1911, after Portugal became a republic in 1910 — it replaced the old blue-and-white flag of the monarchy.

Every part has a meaning:

  • GreenOften linked to hope — it was the colour of the republican movement.
  • RedSaid to recall the blood of those who died for the country.
  • Armillary sphereA golden navigation instrument (esfera armilar) — the symbol of the Discoveries and Portugal’s voyages across the seas.
  • 5 blue shieldsThe quinas, inside the white shield. Legend says they stand for five Moorish kings defeated by Dom Afonso Henriques. The little white dots are old coins (besantes).
  • 7 gold castlesOn the red border — the castles (castelos) taken from the Moors during the Reconquista.

The green part is smaller (2/5) and the red part larger (3/5); the coat of arms sits right on the line between the two colours — over the point of the armillary sphere, showing Portugal looking out to sea.

🌍 The Portuguese language

O português is one of the world’s most spoken languages — around 260 million speakers. It is the official language of:

🇵🇹 Portugal🇧🇷 Brasil🇦🇴 Angola🇲🇿 Moçambique🇨🇻 Cabo Verde🇬🇼 Guiné-Bissau🇸🇹 São Tomé e Príncipe🇹🇱 Timor-Leste

🎭 A little culture

Fado — soulful traditional music (UNESCO heritage). Luís de Camões — the great national poet. Azulejos — the beautiful painted tiles you see on many buildings. Pastéis de nata — famous custard tarts. 😋

Word of the dayPalavra do dia
⭐
alva
the first light of dawn

Meaning: alva is the soft, pale light just before sunrise — the break of day. It also means very white or pure (it is the feminine of alvo).

The morning star: the “estrela d’alva” is the planet Venus — the bright star you can see at dawn.

Why it’s special: alva is an old, poetic word — you meet it in poems and in Fado more than in everyday talk. It carries a feeling of calm, of hope and of a new beginning.

“ao romper da alva”“at the break of dawn” — a classic poetic image
